Before you start

A map, not a sermon.

Most of us live our relationships by accident. This workbook helps you live them on purpose, starting with who you are and who is already in your life.

What you need

  • About 15 minutes somewhere quiet.
  • A pen. Print these pages, or write in a notebook beside them.
  • Honesty. Nobody sees this but you.

Who wrote this

Judah Becker has served for more than 50 years as a minister, missionary, counselor and advocate for justice, in the United States, Southeast Asia and more than two dozen other countries. He holds a Master's degree in Counseling. In 2008 he founded Change A Destiny, which works through local leaders to rescue children from trafficking and help communities stand on their own.

This workbook comes from two chapters of his book, The Path: Transformative Living.

A note on words. Judah is a Messianic Jew: a Jewish follower of Jesus. Sometimes he calls Jesus by His Hebrew name, Yeshua. You don't need any background in Hebrew roots, or in any church, to use this map.

What this is not. This is a faith-based reflection exercise, not therapy, and not a substitute for counseling or crisis care. If you are in crisis in the US, call or text 988.

The idea in one page

M.A.P. means Meaning And Purpose.

Judah's starting point is simple: who you are should shape what you do, not the other way around.

"Being must define doing."Judah Becker, The Path

Your meaning is rooted in who you are. Your purpose shows up in what you give. And both are lived out through people: the ones God has already placed around you.

1
Your name

Who you are, beneath the roles and the labels.

Four questions

What you do, who for, and what each side receives.

3
Your people

Your R.E.M.: Relationship Enrichment Map, then your Five by Five.

"Love without intention disappears. And purpose without people becomes pride."From The Path, Chapter 3

Take the steps in order. If one question stalls you, leave it blank and come back. Blank lines are part of the map too.

Step 1 · About 3 minutes

Start with your name.

Judah asks every traveler to begin here: treasure the name God calls you, not the names other people call you.

My name is
What it means
Look it up if you don't know. Judah's means "praise."
Names I call myself that hurt
Failure. Too much. Not enough. Write the ones you actually hear in your head.
The names I want to trade them for
Healing, hopeful, creative names. Listen carefully; Judah says God whispers your name in love.

Why this comes first: if you skip who you are, the next step turns into a to-do list. Meaning comes before method.

The Path Map4

Step 2 · About 5 minutes

Four questions.

Answer them about your real life today, at work, at home, wherever you spend your hours. Not the life you think you should have.

Judah's own answers: "I am Judah. My name means praise. What do I do? I do justice. I visit widows and orphans. I develop prosperous communities. Who do I do it for? For my King, and for those in poverty and prostitution. What do I get out of it? A deep sense of accomplishment, love, power and wisdom. What do they get out of it? They are set free and live a prosperous, abundant life."

1. What do I do?
2. Who do I do it for?
3. What do I get out of it?
4. What do they get out of it?
The Path Map5

Step 2, continued

Put it in one sentence.

This is your M.A.P. It will change as you do. Write it in pencil if that helps.

An example from the book. Elias repairs furniture at a small community center. His first instinct was to write "for my paycheck." Then he remembered Marcus, an angry thirteen-year-old who stayed late to learn carving, and Mrs. Alvarez, whose kitchen table he fixed after her husband died. He wrote:

"I build and restore what has been broken, for the Lord and for those who need restoration. I receive joy, peace and purpose. They receive hope, dignity, and a reminder that broken things can be made whole."

I am
I (what you do)
for (who you do it for)
I receive
They receive
"My meaning is rooted in who I am. My purpose is revealed in what I give."Adapted from The Path, Chapter 14

Where this goes

You just took the first step.

In the book, the M.A.P. and R.E.M. are the doorway to a longer road. Judah lays it out in three sections.

Levels 1–3
Prayer

Learning to pray sincerely, then for your people, then for projects and missions.

You are here
Levels 4–6
Undertakings

Picking one real project, working on it with prayer partners, then helping lead it.

Levels 7–9
Power

Forming teams, training others, and living as what Judah calls a justice minister.

If you want to keep going

None of these is required. This map is yours either way.
