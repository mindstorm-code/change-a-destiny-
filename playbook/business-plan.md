## THE PROBLEM

Millions of people raised in evangelical/charismatic churches have walked
away from the institution without walking away from their faith — roughly
40 million Americans, per *The Great Dechurching* (2023). They still
pray, still believe, but no longer trust the building. At the same time,
church leaders can't meet the clinical need: 52% of practicing Christians
want their church to offer real counseling, but only 31% of pastors feel
equipped to provide it (Barna). That leaves people stuck between two
systems that don't talk to each other — a spiritual framework with no
clinical rigor, or clinical therapy with no room for faith.

Layered on top of that: people who care about causes like anti-human-
trafficking are giving less, not because they stopped caring, but because
the scale of the problem makes any single gift feel meaningless
("efficacy paralysis" — well-documented in donor psychology). They don't
need a bigger ask. They need a bounded one they can actually see work.

## THE SYSTEM

We run one connected path, not three separate products:

**Prove It (The Book)**
*The Path: Transformative Living* ($9.10, Amazon) — a same-day read that
proves the framework and Judah's voice before anyone is asked for real
money.

**Transform (The Founding Cohort)**
A 6-week paid course ($497–$1,497) — the actual clinical-and-spiritual
integration framework, delivered live, not just read.

**Act (Change A Destiny / Victorious Villages)**
A self-sustaining anti-trafficking rescue and rehabilitation model
already operating in India, Cambodia, and Thailand — introduced *inside*
the course, not competing for attention on the same page. Healed people
become generous people; this is proof-driven, not guilt-driven.

These depend on each other. The book earns the right to ask for cohort
tuition. The cohort earns the trust to introduce the mission. Pull one
out and the rest gets weaker — a course with no proof-step in front of it
converts worse; a mission with no transformation story behind it reads as
one more guilt-based ask.
